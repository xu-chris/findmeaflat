# Product Vision — From Notifier to Flat-Hunting Intelligence

The rewrite started as "make the crawler work again and stop running five instances".
The scope you described is larger and, I think, better: **a German flat-hunting tool
that knows things the portals will not tell you.**

This document separates that vision into what it is, what makes it defensible, and what
should be built when. `OPEN-DATA.md` carries the evidence behind every data claim here.

---

## 1. The repositioning

**Today:** a personal crawler that forwards new listings to one Telegram chat.
Its value is *speed* — you see a listing before the portal's own email digest goes out.

**Proposed: the data crawler for the German housing problem.** A notifier is the surface;
the substance is a longitudinal, cross-portal dataset of who is offering what, at what
price relative to the legal reference, through which management company, and who is
looking for it.

| | What it answers | Why a portal will never build it |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed** | "There is a new flat, right now" | They do build this — it is the commodity half |
| **Price truth** | "Is this rent legal and fair, per the official Mietspiegel?" | Portals are paid by landlords. Flagging listings as 40 % over reference is directly against their revenue |
| **Credibility** | "Is this listing a scam?" | Portals under-police this; admitting a fraud rate is bad for the brand |
| **Reachability** | "Who actually manages this building, and what is their number?" | Portals monetise the contact channel. Routing renters around it destroys their funnel |
| **Supply memory** | "This Verwaltung has listed 43 flats in Wedding this year" | Requires longitudinal cross-portal data they have no reason to expose |
| **Demand memory** | "What are people actually searching for, and at what price?" | Nobody has this. It exists only in a tool where users declare their criteria |

Speed is table stakes that gets people in. Everything below it is the product.

**The demand side is the least obvious and possibly the most valuable.** Every `Search`
row is a declared statement of what someone wants: this city, these districts, this
size, this budget. Across users that aggregates into something no portal, no statistics
office and no Mietspiegel captures — *unmet demand*. Which price points have no supply,
which districts are oversubscribed, how far below asking rents real budgets sit.

This was checked against the state's actual data holdings, and the result is clean-cut:
**the German state already buys supply data** (BBSR purchases ImmoScout24 listings via
empirica-systeme, now VALUE AG, part of Hypoport), **but nobody observes demand** — the
BBSR's *Wohnungsbedarfsprognose* is demographic modelling from the Zensus 2022, and the
BBSR has a live 2025 research programme conceding its rent-data foundations are
inadequate. Full research, the buyer landscape, the GDPR design constraints and the
sell-versus-publish fork are in `DEMAND-DATA.md`.

The load-bearing conclusion: **supply data is unsellable (scraped provenance fails
public procurement) and the market is taken; demand data is clean, uncontested and
generated by your own users.** It is also the most sensitive data in the system —
aggregate only, k-anonymity thresholds, disclosed in the bot, never per-person.

**Why this is defensible.** The moat is not the crawler — crawlers are easy and break
constantly. The moat is **the accumulated dataset**: every listing ever seen, joined to
official Mietspiegel values, joined to buildings, joined to the commercial providers
that keep reappearing. That compounds daily and cannot be bought. It also means the
right architectural instinct is *store everything you parse, forever* — which the
current `db/listing.json` (an array of bare IDs) throws away.

## 2. The four capabilities

### 2.1 Fair-rent check — the flagship

For a listing at a known address, compute the ortsübliche Vergleichsmiete and compare.

```
Listing:  1.450 € kalt · 62 m² · Wedding · Baujahr ~1905 · Balkon, kein Aufzug
            ↓
Wohnlage (Berlin WFS, per address)          → "mittel"
Baualtersklasse (Umweltatlas, block level)  → "bis 1918"
Wohnflächenklasse                            → "60–70 m²"
Ausstattung (parsed from listing prose)      → +Balkon, −Aufzug   [low confidence]
            ↓
Mietspiegel reference    ~11,20 €/m²
This listing             23,39 €/m²   → +109 %
```

Delivered inside the existing Telegram message, not as a separate feature:

> **Helle 2-Zimmer-Wohnung im Wedding**
> 📍 Müllerstraße 12, 13353 Berlin
> 💶 1.450 € kalt · 📐 62 m² · 🛌 2 Zimmer
> 📊 **+109 % über Mietspiegel** (Referenz ~11,20 €/m², Wohnlage *mittel*, Bj. bis 1918)
> ⚠️ Ab +10 % kann die Mietpreisbremse greifen — verbindlich prüft das der Mieterverein
> 🏢 Meier Immobilienverwaltung GmbH · ☎️ 030 12345678
> [Zum Inserat](…) · [Google Maps](…)

**Constraints to be honest about, in the product and not just in the docs:**

- Equipment features come from marketing prose and are the weakest input. Show a
  confidence level; do not present a guess as a measurement.
- Machine-readable Mietspiegel exists for **Berlin (Wohnlagen) and Dortmund** today.
  Everywhere else needs a hand-digitised table. Cities without one must degrade to
  "no reference available", never to a silently wrong number.
- The output is informational. Framing it as a legal verdict is both wrong and a
  liability — see `OPEN-DATA.md` §4.

**This single feature also improves the core product**, which is the part worth
noticing: it becomes a *filter*. "Only notify me about listings within 15 % of the
Mietspiegel" is a fundamentally better filter than a price ceiling, because it adapts to
the neighbourhood. That is the answer to your question about what combination of signals
to filter on: **relative to reference, not absolute.**

### 2.2 Building intelligence

Once listings are geocoded to a building, the address becomes a join key:

- Wohnlage classification (official, per address, back to 2003 → trajectory)
- Building age class (block level)
- Denkmalschutz status (OSM `heritage` / `ref:lda` for Berlin)
- Building height / storeys (Umweltatlas, OSM `building:levels`)
- **Your own history: every listing ever seen at this address**

That last line is the one nobody else can produce. "This flat was listed three times in
18 months at rising rents" is a strong signal about a landlord, and it is free once
listings are stored properly.

### 2.3 Provider and Hausverwaltung directory

Built from the Anbieter field already present in scraped HTML, enriched from public
Impressum data (§5 DDG makes it mandatory and public) and the Handelsregister.

- Phone number → the user calls instead of joining a 50-message queue
- Listing count and areas of activity, from your own crawl history
- Median deviation from Mietspiegel across their listings — a genuine reputation signal

**Hard boundary: commercial providers only.** A GmbH's Impressum is public company
data. A private individual landlord's details are personal data, and building a
database of them triggers GDPR Art. 14's duty to proactively notify every person
scraped — not satisfiable at scale. Classify the Anbieter; store companies, skip
individuals. Reasoning in `OPEN-DATA.md` §5.

### 2.4 Contact strategy

Your diagnosis is right — landlords stop reading around message ten — but the three
mechanisms you floated have very different risk profiles and should not ship together.

| Mechanism | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Be fast + hand the user a pre-filled message to send themselves** | **Build.** Attacks the real problem, breaks nothing |
| **Surface the Hausverwaltung's phone number** | **Build.** The strongest idea in the set |
| Auto-submitting the platform's contact form | **Do not build** |
| Delayed re-send to bump to the top of the landlord's inbox | **Do not build** |

On the two rejections, briefly: auto-submission violates every portal's terms, and on
ImmoScout24 it is behind the same WAF challenge the crawler already cannot pass — so it
is blocked *and* prohibited. Beyond that, if this tool becomes popular in Germany,
mass auto-replies make the flooding worse for every renter including your own users;
the equilibrium is landlords abandoning platform messages entirely. Inbox-bumping is
deliberate manipulation of the platform's ordering and backfires socially — a duplicate
message reads as pushy, not persistent.

The legitimate version is stronger anyway. A 2-minute crawl plus instant push makes
your user message **#3 instead of #47**, and a complete, well-structured first message
measurably improves response rates. The bot knows the flat's details and the user's
standing profile; merging them into a paste-ready message takes seconds. The user
presses send. That wins the same race without entering the arms race.

## 3. Sequencing

The vision does not change the first bet — it changes what the first bet must not
foreclose.

**Phase 1 — Working notifier, multi-user.** Everything in `TARGET-ARCHITECTURE.md`.
Crawling fixed, one deployment serving you and your sister, self-service setup in
Telegram. **Nothing here is optional**, because every later capability needs listings
flowing and addresses stored.

The one change Phase 1 must absorb: **store full listing data, not just IDs.** The
`Listing` resource keeps title, address, price, size, rooms, raw parse output, and
timestamps, forever. Cheap now, and the entire dataset thesis depends on it.

**Phase 2 — Address resolution.** Geocode listings to buildings; ingest Berlin
Wohnlagen. Deliverable: `/wohnlage <address>` answers correctly. Low risk, and it
proves the join key works before anything is built on it.

**Phase 3 — Fair-rent check, Berlin only.** Digitise the Berlin Mietspiegel table,
parse equipment features from prose with confidence scoring, add the comparison line to
notifications. Add the `max_percent_over_mietspiegel` filter. **This is the release
that changes what the product is.**

**Phase 4 — Provider directory.** Extract and classify Anbieter, enrich commercial ones
from Impressum, surface phone numbers and history in messages.

**Phase 4b — Credibility.** Scam scoring, per `CREDIBILITY.md`. Sequenced here because
its strongest signal — price far *below* the Mietspiegel reference — is the negative
tail of the number Phase 3 already computes, and its second-strongest — photo reuse
across portals — needs the image URLs Phase 1 must capture. Cheap once both exist,
impossible without them.

**Phase 5 — Expansion and interfaces.** Dortmund (data already exists), then cities by
demand. The **MCP server** via `ash_ai`, exposing the dataset as tools: reference rent
for an address, listings by Mietspiegel deviation, provider lookup and history,
credibility assessment. Aggregate demand reporting in the admin surface.

Phases 2–5 are separate bets, each shaped and grilled on its own. This document exists
so Phase 1 does not accidentally block them, not to commit to them now.

## 4. What the expanded vision changes about Phase 1

Seven concrete adjustments, all cheap now and expensive later:

1. **`Listing` stores everything, permanently.** Full attributes plus the raw parse map.
   No pruning. The dataset is the product.
2. **Capture `description` in full and `image_urls`.** These are the only two fields
   that are *unrecoverable* — a delisted listing takes its text and photos with it, and
   `CREDIBILITY.md` §4 depends on both. Everything else can be derived from stored data
   whenever it is built. This is the single most important line in this document.
3. **Address as a structured field**, with `latitude`/`longitude` and a nullable
   `building_id` from the start. Retro-geocoding a year of free-text addresses is
   painful; capturing it at parse time is free.
4. **A fifth domain reserved: `FindMeAFlat.Reference`** — `MietspiegelTable`,
   `MietspiegelEntry`, `Wohnlage`, `Building`, `Provider`, `CredibilityAssessment`.
   Empty in Phase 1, but its existence keeps enrichment out of `Listings`.
5. **`Filter` as its own resource, not columns on `Search`.** Phase 3 introduces filter
   kinds that are not simple comparisons (`percent_over_reference`); Phase 4b adds
   `max_credibility_risk`. Modelling filters as rows now avoids a migration touching
   every user's searches later.
6. **Locale from the first message.** The audience is German. `ex_gram` receives
   Telegram's `language_code`; store it and default the bot to German. Retrofitting i18n
   after the message templates exist is the classic avoidable rewrite.
7. **Enable `postgis` and `vector` in the first migration.** Nothing uses them in
   Phase 1. Adding extensions to a live database later is a migration nobody enjoys, and
   `ash_ai` vectorisation and radius search are both on the roadmap.
8. **Make searches temporal and non-destructive.** Timestamp every `Search` and `Filter`
   state change, soft-delete rather than hard-delete, record per-period match counts
   (searches returning *nothing* are the demand dataset's most valuable number), store
   the consent decision with its wording version, and key geography to district/LOR
   rather than free-text city names. `DEMAND-DATA.md` §6 explains why each is expensive
   to retrofit and cheap now.

## 5. Decided, and still open

**Decided:**

- **Users get Telegram only.** No user-facing web UI, permanently. This removes user
  authentication, sessions and password flows from the product entirely.
- **Admin is Phoenix/LiveView only, behind AshAuthentication**, plus `ash_admin`.
- **MCP server via `ash_ai`** in the near future, authenticated by API key or OAuth 2.1.
- **Mietspiegel: current edition only.** Berlin's historic Wohnlagen series (2003→) is
  skipped; it is a research capability, not a product one.
- **Credibility scoring is in scope**, sequenced as Phase 4b.

**Still open, and not blocking Phase 1:**

1. **Who is this for?** Still the biggest one. "You, your sister and friends" and "a
   popular German product" are different systems — the second brings GDPR controller
   duties, abuse handling, support and hosting cost. The architecture supports both;
   the operating posture does not.
2. **Sell the demand statistics, or publish them?** The two paths diverge hard on
   exclusivity, and `DEMAND-DATA.md` §5 argues publishing may be the stronger play — it
   converts the funding conversation from procurement (tendered, hostile to small
   operators) into Förderung, and sidesteps provenance entirely. Does not need deciding
   now; does need deciding before the first buyer conversation.
3. **Is there a business model?** Three candidates now: freemium (free notifications,
   paid Mietspiegel check), B2G/B2B demand statistics per `DEMAND-DATA.md`, or grant
   funding for an open index. All interact with licensing — this repo is MIT and forked
   from `adriankumpf/findmeaflat`.
4. **How much Mietspiegel digitisation are you willing to do by hand?** The gating cost
   for every city beyond Berlin and Dortmund. No amount of engineering removes it.
5. **How exposed do you want to be on the portals' terms?** A private tool for five
   people and a public German product are judged very differently, particularly on
   ImmoScout24's WAF.
